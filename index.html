Dark Matter:

Dark matter is a mysterious type of matter that does not emit, reflect, or absorb light, making it invisible to telescopes. Scientists cannot see dark matter directly, but they know it exists because of its gravitational effects on stars, galaxies, and other objects in space. It is believed to make up about 85% of all the matter in the universe, far more than the ordinary matter that forms stars, planets, and living things.

Dark matter is needed because many observations of the universe cannot be explained by visible matter alone. For example, stars in galaxies move much faster than expected based on the amount of matter that can be seen. Without additional unseen mass, these galaxies would fly apart. Dark matter also helps explain how galaxies formed and why large groups of galaxies remain held together by gravity.

Scientists have observed the effects of dark matter by studying the motion of stars and galaxies, as well as a phenomenon called gravitational lensing, where gravity bends light from distant objects. These observations show that there is much more mass present than can be detected with telescopes. Although dark matter has never been observed directly, its gravitational influence provides strong evidence that it exists throughout the universe.
